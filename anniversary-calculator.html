Traditional vs. Modern: Why Two Lists?

The short version

  • Traditional gifts date back generations — paper, cotton, leather, wood, tin, silver, gold. They're symbolic: paper for a fresh start, silver for a bond that's hardened, gold for a love that's truly precious.
  • Modern gifts were added in the 20th century to feel more practical — clocks, silverware, appliances, watches, diamonds.
  • Either is right. Many couples mix both: a silver picture frame (traditional) with a modern experience, or diamond earrings (modern 10th) presented in a tin keepsake box.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the traditional gift for a 10th anniversary?

Tin or aluminum is the traditional 10th anniversary gift — symbolizing flexibility and durability that a decade of marriage builds. The modern 10th-anniversary gift is diamond.

What are the traditional anniversary gifts by year?

The classic list starts with paper (year 1), cotton (2), leather (3), fruit & flowers (4), wood (5), then milestones like tin/aluminum (10), crystal (15), china (20), silver (25), ruby (40), and gold (50), running all the way to diamond at 60 and 75.

What is the modern gift for a 25th anniversary?

Silver — the same as the traditional gift, marking the "silver" milestone. Modern anniversary lists pair each year with a contemporary material such as clocks, silverware, watches, or diamonds.

Is there an anniversary gift for every year?

Standard lists define gifts for years 1 through 25, then every fifth year (30, 35, 40, 45, 50 and beyond). For years without a set traditional gift, a personalized present — jewelry, a photo book, or a shared experience — is the norm.
