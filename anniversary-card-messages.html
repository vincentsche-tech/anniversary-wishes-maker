How to Write an Anniversary Card Message

✅ What Works in a Card

  • Name the milestone — "Happy 10th!" lands better than a generic greeting
  • Pick one specific thing to praise or remember, not a whole list
  • End with a forward-looking line: here's to the year ahead
  • Leave room to sign — a cramped signature looks rushed

What Makes a Card Different From a Text

A card is physical, which changes what works. It gets kept — in a drawer, on a shelf, taped inside a wardrobe door — so the message should be something worth re-reading in five years.

That also means less is more. Two or three handwritten lines fill a card nicely; a full paragraph turns into a cramped scrawl. If you have more to say, write the long version as a letter or send it later, and let the card carry the headline.

Handwriting matters too. Print if your handwriting is hard to read — the point is that they can read it, not that it looks impressive.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I write in an anniversary card?

Keep it to two or three lines: name the milestone, say one thing you love or admire, and sign off warmly. A card usually gets kept for years, so one sincere sentence beats a long paragraph.

How long should an anniversary card message be?

Two to four lines is the sweet spot for most cards. That is enough for a real sentiment without crowding the page — if you have more to say, say it in person and let the card hold the headline.

Should I sign an anniversary card with both names?

If the card is from you and your partner or family, yes — sign both names so the couple knows who is celebrating them. If it is from you alone, sign only your own name to avoid confusion.
